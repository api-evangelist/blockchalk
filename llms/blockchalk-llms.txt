# BlockChalk

> BlockChalk was a location-based neighborhood messaging service (2009-2012). Users with
> GPS-enabled phones left short public messages called "chalks" pinned to a geographic point,
> and read the chalks other people had left nearby. The company was founded by Stephen Hood
> (former Delicious product lead) and Dave Baggeroer (Stanford Institute of Design), and
> raised $1M in seed funding in May 2010 from Joshua Schachter, Battery Ventures, Founder
> Collective, Harrison Metal, Mitch Kapor and others.

IMPORTANT: BlockChalk is RETIRED. The product is gone and blockchalk.com does not resolve —
the domain registration is active through 2028 but its delegated nameservers return no
records. There is no live API. Everything in this repository is historical documentation
reconstructed from the provider's own archived developer page and from captured live
responses. Do not attempt to integrate with BlockChalk.

## Historical API surface

BlockChalk published three read-only, anonymous interfaces on its /developers page, all
parameterized by a longitude/latitude pair:

- XML: http://blockchalk.com/api/xml/chalk?long=[longitude]&lat=[latitude]
- JSON: http://blockchalk.com/api/json/chalk?long=[longitude]&lat=[latitude]
- RSS: http://blockchalk.com/rss/chalk?long=[longitude]&lat=[latitude]

A later versioned path is evidenced by captured 200 responses:

- GeoRSS: http://blockchalk.com/api/v0.6/chalks/[latitude],[longitude]

BlockChalk's own caveat on that page: "these interfaces are early prototypes and are likely
to change in the future."

There was no authentication, no API key, no registration, no pagination, no rate-limit
signaling, no documented error contract, no HTTPS endpoint, and no write API. Chalks were
posted through the mobile clients only.

## Data shape

A chalk is a feed item carrying a title (the message text), a permalink and matching guid
using a 12-character hex id, an RFC 1123 pubDate, a georss:point with latitude and longitude,
and a description holding a human-readable proximity label such as "on this block", "within
a few blocks" or a named neighborhood like "in South of Market".
